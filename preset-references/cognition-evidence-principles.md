# Cognition evidence principles

## Subjective reports and stable evidence

A person's retrospective account is the most important calibration signal for
their own intent, but it is not automatically a complete objective record.
Interpretation must keep these evidence levels separate:

- motives the person explicitly recognizes and confirms;
- secondary motives the person considers possible but uncertain;
- hypotheses supported by behavior but not yet consciously confirmed;
- model-only guesses that currently have no supporting evidence.

Do not dismiss a person's account by claiming that they do not understand
themselves. Do not make every possible interpretation true merely to agree
with them. A stable conclusion requires repeated support across different
wording, relationship stages, or preceding events. One scene creates a
provisional hypothesis, not a permanent keyword-to-intent rule.
